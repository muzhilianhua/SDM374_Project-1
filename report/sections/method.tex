\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
The policy sees the 2,048 training rows and the results of its own calls, nothing else. It keeps the incumbent
$W^\ast$ (the best point measured so far; initially the best training row, which is never resent) and proposes 50 new
points per call.

\subsection{Additive Block Descent}
\label{sec:block}
Let $B_t$ be the \emph{base} of call $t$ and $B_t[b\!\leftarrow\!w]$ the base with block $b$ replaced by $w$. Every
candidate except the combinations below has this single-block form, so its measured effect
$\delta_b(w)=R(B_t[b\!\leftarrow\!w])-R(B_t)$ belongs to one building. Each building keeps a table $v_b$ that gives
every measured block an additive value, anchored at $0$ for that building's block of the best training row; a
single-block point adds $v_b(w)=v_b(B_t[b])+\delta_b(w)$. Under additivity, $\delta_b$ does not depend on the other
blocks of the base, so values measured at different bases are comparable, and
\begin{equation}
\hat R(w_1,w_2,w_3)=R(W^\ast)+\textstyle\sum_{b=1}^{3}\bigl(v_b(w_b)-v_b(w^\ast_b)\bigr),
\label{eq:add}
\end{equation}
which needs no model fit. The base of the next call is the combination with the lowest $\hat R$ among the incumbent's
block and the two best improving blocks of each building; up to six further combinations are sent with it. Base and
combination points change several buildings at once, and they are chosen by \eqref{eq:add}, not by the surrogate. The
design gives three properties at once: every point is an attributable experiment, one batch advances three
6-dimensional simplex searches (7 weights each) in parallel, and improvements found in different buildings are composed at the cost of one
point each. It is a block-coordinate descent~\cite{bcd} that uses additivity as additive Bayesian optimisation
does~\cite{addgp}, but with measured rather than modelled block effects.

Within a block, candidates come from three slot classes with fixed shares 50/25/25 in v1 and v2: \emph{surrogate} picks, the lowest lower confidence bound
(mean $-$ std) among 1,500 perturbations, from a ridge trend plus an ARD GP on the 600 rows nearest the base;
\emph{random} moves at a per-building trust scale that grows by 1.5 after a success and shrinks by 0.6 after a failure,
within $[0.01,1]$~\cite{turbo}; \emph{fine} moves (small multiplicative steps, zeroing a minor weight, or a tiny share
between the 0.1\,\% and 10\,\% quantiles of the training weights, $1.6\times10^{-6}$ to $7.2\times10^{-3}$). From
call 13, half of the fine slots are one-coordinate log-grid \emph{scans}. In calls 1--6, v2 adds three \emph{shift}
moves per building outside the shares, biased towards objectives whose share correlates negatively with $R$ in the
training rows; call 1 of v1 and v2 also holds eight multi-building jumps built from surrogate-predicted blocks.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One call of the policy (v4)}
\label{alg:call}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require training rows, this run's measured points, seed, call index $t$
\State $W^\ast\gets$ best measured point; update block values $v_b$ (anchored as above), trust scales, null intervals, hit counts $h_c,n_c$
\State $B_t\gets\arg\min\hat R$ over combinations of each building's two best improving blocks~\eqref{eq:add}
\State batch $\gets\{B_t\}\,\cup$ up to six next-best combinations
\State compute class shares $s_c$ by~\eqref{eq:share}
\For{building $b=1,2,3$ and class $c$}
\State draw $s_c n_b$ blocks $w$ of class $c$ from $B_t[b]$ ($n_b$: free slots of $b$, rounded), skipping null intervals and sent points
\State batch $\gets$ batch $\cup\,\{B_t[b\!\leftarrow\!w]\}$
\EndFor
\State send the 50 points; record every $R$ \Comment{randomness seeded by (seed, $t$)}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Evidence-Driven Allocation}
\label{sec:alloc}
Fixed class shares spend most slots where little is found: with shares 50/25/25 the surrogate held 44\,\% of the
points of v2 but hit only 3.5\,\% of the time. Variant v4 makes scans a class of their own from call 9, drops the
call-1 jumps (no hit in any rehearsal), and adds three rules that use the run's own results only.
\emph{Plateau filter:} a one-coordinate step that returns exactly $R(B_t)$ ($|\delta|\le10^{-9}$) is null; for each
building, base block and coordinate, no later step is proposed inside the range spanned by the null steps found so
far, and a new base block starts with no such range. Scans step just outside this range and re-apply the previous
call's improving coordinates to the new base.
\emph{Posterior shares:} with $h_c$ hits (points below their call's base) among $n_c$ points of class $c$ over the last
four calls,
\begin{equation}
s_c=f_c+\Bigl(1-\textstyle\sum_{c'}f_{c'}\Bigr)\frac{r_c}{\sum_{c'}r_{c'}},\qquad r_c=\frac{h_c+1}{n_c+5},
\label{eq:share}
\end{equation}
a Beta(1,4) posterior mean with floors $f_c=0.25$ for surrogate picks and 0.10 otherwise, so no class is starved.
\emph{No repeats:} a weight vector equal to a training row or a sent point is never proposed, so every call sends 50
new weight vectors; distinct vectors can still return the same $R$ on a plateau.

\subsection{Deployment}
\label{sec:deploy}
The submitted policy (h7) is v4 for calls 1--11; from call 12 it replaces up to 12 fine or zeroing slots by coarser
single-coordinate, multi-coordinate and restart moves. All randomness derives from (seed, call), and the graded
package pins and checks the BLAS kernel and thread count, because the GP hyperparameter fit amplifies rounding differences into
different selections. The package writes each batch to disk before sending it, never resends a charged call, and
can recompute every batch from its log without the simulator.
